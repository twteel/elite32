# Court Light — design philosophy

**Darkness as the room, light as the subject.** Every cover is a dim gym after the
final whistle: the crowd is still there, pushed deep into black, grain and grunge
settling over it like dust caught in a floodlight. Nothing in the background is
allowed to compete. It is atmosphere, not information, and it is graded with the
patience of a darkroom printer, burned down until only the shapes of people remain.

**One face, lit.** The guest is pulled out of that darkness in pure black and white,
large, close, unretouched in spirit, carried by contrast instead of effects. No
outlines, no stickers, no colour washes. A soft fall-off of shadow separates them
from the crowd so they read as lit from the front, the way a single camera light
finds a speaker. The face is the only organic form on the canvas; everything else is
geometry built around it, placed with painstaking care.

**Type as structure.** Words are set in an extended, heavy grotesk, the voice of the
wordmark, and sit on solid blocks like signage bolted to a wall. Black blocks carry
the setup, a single pink block (#EC3767) carries the punch. That pink is the only
colour on the canvas and it appears exactly once at full strength, so it lands every
time. The hook is two to five words. It is never a sentence.

**Systematic quiet.** Around the loud type, a second voice: small monospaced labels,
episode numbers, thin rules, corner marks: the clinical markings of an archive
documenting something that happened. They are tiny, aligned to one grid and evenly
spaced, and they give the composition its sense of order. Margins are generous and
identical on every side; nothing touches an edge it was not meant to touch.

**Made by hand, at scale.** The system renders hundreds of covers, so every rule must
be one a master designer would sign: optical alignment over mathematical alignment,
blocks that hug their type with exact padding, a rhythm that holds whether the hook
is one word or five. Each cover should look as if it took hours of meticulous
adjustment. The craft is in the restraint.
